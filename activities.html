Taiwan Global Pathfinders Initiative Program

青年百億海外圓夢基金計畫 · approximately NT$280,000 in government funding

I independently prepared the application and proposal, then completed preparatory training at the National Museum of Marine Biology and Aquarium (NMMBA) in Taiwan and an overseas learning program at the Hawaii Institute of Marine Biology (HIMB) on Coconut Island.

At NMMBA, I studied coral identification, marine ecology, bleaching, restoration, and research methods. At HIMB, I learned about coral nursery and propagation work, coral-health observation, water-quality monitoring, CoralWatch, Structure-from-Motion, 3D mapping, GIS and R, ReefCloud, and drone or aerial monitoring concepts. I also attended the Hawaii Conservation Conference and earned PADI Open Water certification.

What changed for me: The program showed me how environmental science can work with engineering, sensing, data, mapping, AI, and computer vision in marine conservation.

AI Fish Detection & Aquarium Monitoring

NYCU Adaptive Photonics Laboratory, Tainan · school coral-tank system

During a summer 2026 learning experience at National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University's Adaptive Photonics Laboratory, I worked mainly under the day-to-day guidance of a senior lab member. I practiced bounding-box annotation, class mapping, train-validation preparation, model training, and YOLO detection using underwater and field fish imagery associated with marine research and NMMBA-related data.

I then built an independent 8-class dataset for the school coral tank: 419 images and approximately 5,100 bounding boxes. A first model worked relatively well on controlled data, but full-tank video exposed false positives caused by pipes, equipment, reflections, and background structures.
